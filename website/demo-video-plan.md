# Demo video plan (target 2:30 to 3:00)

Goal: show a real drummer changing tempo and the rig following, with lights. Copy rules (RS-01 section 9): tagline
"The band sets the tempo. The machine keeps up."; do not use the word "click" in positive copy (use "tempo", "beat",
"count-in"); name no competitor in the voiceover; on-screen comparison is the table on the website only if re-verified.

## Kit and crew

- Drummer (not a session player who needs a click; someone who drifts musically), kit with kick, snare and overhead mics.
- Laptop running Pacemaker standalone (or plugin in Live), audio interface, MIDI interface to one hardware box (drum
  machine or sequencer), a lighting or VJ app on a second laptop on the same wired network.
- 2 cameras (wide, tight on drummer), 1 screen capture, 1 phone on the hardware box, audio from the PA mix plus a
  separate stem recording of drums and backing.
- Prepare: stage test checklist (docs/stage-test-checklist.md) run first so every shot is known to work.

## Capture and technical notes

- Record Pacemaker's drift log during the shoot; the report shot (12) comes from the real session.
- Sync picture and audio with a slate clap; keep a separate drums-only stem to prove the tempo change.
- Show real screens, no mock-ups. If a shot does not work live, reshoot it; do not fake.
- Use a wired network for Link and OSC. Show peer count on screen at least once.
- Captions in the lower third for tempo digits so viewers without sound still follow the story.
- Music: drummer's own playing plus the backing track used in the demo; clear all rights.
- Do not show any trademarked host logos unless the licence terms allow; text names are enough.
